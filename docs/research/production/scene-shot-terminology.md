## 1. Purpose

This record clarifies production terminology boundaries so AI Drama System can preserve Story -> Episode -> Scene -> Shot while still supporting discipline-specific language used in script, boards, and editorial.

## 2. Professional Terminology

- Established terminology (screenwriting): Scene heading (slug line) marks one scene and includes INT/EXT, location, and time of day. Source: https://www.finaldraft.com/learn/screenplay-formatting-elements/
- Established terminology (film grammar education): Scene is part of story at a particular time and location. Source: https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling/film-grammar/a/glossary-grammar
- Common practice (film grammar education): Shot is a visual representation of character action in time. Source: https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling/film-grammar/a/glossary-grammar
- Common practice (film grammar education): Sequences are collections of scenes that make up a major beat. Source: https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling/film-grammar/a/glossary-grammar
- Common practice (story development): Major beats and minor beats represent narrative rhythm at different scales. Source: https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling/film-grammar/a/glossary-grammar
- Common practice (shot scale): Wide shots can function as establishing shots for location/context orientation. Source: https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling/film-grammar/a/glossary-grammar
- Studio/software-specific terminology: Storyboard Pro organizes work with scenes and panels and then animatic timing. Source: https://docs.toonboom.com/help/storyboard-pro-27/storyboard/getting-started/introduction.html
- Studio/software-specific terminology: Storyboard Pro includes panel timing, camera, sound, and export tooling. Source: https://docs.toonboom.com/help/storyboard-pro-27/storyboard/book/user-guide/about-user-guide.html and https://docs.toonboom.com/help/storyboard-pro-27/storyboard/release-notes/storyboard-pro-27-release-notes.html

## 3. Professional Definitions

- Scene (stable enough for domain design): A narrative unit tied to a time/place context in script and planning. Source: https://www.finaldraft.com/learn/screenplay-formatting-elements/ and https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling/film-grammar/a/glossary-grammar
- Shot (stable enough for domain design): A visual execution unit used by film grammar and planning vocabulary. Source: https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling/film-grammar/a/glossary-grammar
- Sequence (discipline-variant): A grouping level above scenes in some writing/planning practice. Source: https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling/film-grammar/a/glossary-grammar and https://www.finaldraft.com/learn/going-from-outline-to-script/
- Beat (discipline-variant): Narrative moment scale used in planning and pacing. Source: https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling/film-grammar/a/glossary-grammar
- Establishing shot (common practice): Often implemented as wide shot to orient location/scale. Source: https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling/film-grammar/a/glossary-grammar

Notes on unresolved terms in this source set:
- Take, setup, master shot, insert, and cutaway are common production terms, but this record marks their exact definitions as cross-discipline variable until additional guild/studio manuals are archived in a later iteration.

## 4. Typical Production Workflow

- Script defines scene boundaries using scene headings. Source: https://www.finaldraft.com/learn/screenplay-formatting-elements/
- Storyboard planning decomposes scenes into visual units and timing-ready panels/animatic elements. Source: https://docs.toonboom.com/help/storyboard-pro-27/storyboard/getting-started/introduction.html
- Film grammar decisions choose shot type/framing/motion per beat. Source: https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling/film-grammar/v/film-grammar-overview
- Editorial maps and refines these units through cut/review workflows. Source: https://help.autodesk.com/view/SGSUB/ENU/

## 5. Roles and Responsibilities

- Screenwriter: defines scene structure in screenplay format. Source: https://www.finaldraft.com/learn/screenplay-formatting-elements/

- Storyboard artist/editorial collaborators: convert board structure to timing-driven animatic/review assets. Source: https://docs.toonboom.com/help/storyboard-pro-27/storyboard/getting-started/introduction.html and https://docs.toonboom.com/help/storyboard-pro-27/storyboard/release-notes/storyboard-pro-27-release-notes.html

## 10. Relationships to Other Production Concepts

- Story: scenes partition story progression. Source: https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling/story-structure/v/piab-storystructure
- Scene: container for related beats and shot decisions.
- Shot: visual execution unit linked to camera grammar.
- Storyboard: translates scene intent into shot visuals/timing.
- Continuity: tracks consistency across shot/scene transitions.
- Edit: final timing/ordering is validated in editorial review. Source: https://help.autodesk.com/view/SGSUB/ENU/

## 11. Stable Industry Concepts

- Script scenes are time/place anchored.
- Shot language is distinct from scene language.
- Beat/sequence terms operate at planning scales above or across shots.
- Wide/establishing usage often introduces geography/context.

## 12. Studio-Specific or Variable Practices

- Panel, scene, and timing terminology in Storyboard Pro is tool-specific and not a universal ontology. Source: https://docs.toonboom.com/help/storyboard-pro-27/storyboard/getting-started/introduction.html
- Sequence/beat granularity varies across studios and departments. Source: https://www.finaldraft.com/learn/going-from-outline-to-script/
- Definitions of take/setup/master shot/insert/cutaway can vary by camera, AD, and editorial culture (OPEN QUESTION in this layer).

## 13. Research Findings

- Finding: Authoritative script formatting uses explicit scene headings for location/time segmentation. Source: https://www.finaldraft.com/learn/screenplay-formatting-elements/
- Finding: Film grammar source defines scene, shot, sequence, beats, and shot classes distinctly. Source: https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling/film-grammar/a/glossary-grammar
- Finding: Storyboard production software emphasizes scene/panel structure, timing, camera, and animatic conversion. Source: https://docs.toonboom.com/help/storyboard-pro-27/storyboard/getting-started/introduction.html and https://docs.toonboom.com/help/storyboard-pro-27/storyboard/book/user-guide/about-user-guide.html

## 14. Research Principles

- Principle: Scene and Shot should remain separate production concepts.
- Principle: Planning-scale terms (beat/sequence) should inform planning, not replace canonical production units.
- Principle: Terminology from one software package should be treated as variable practice unless cross-validated.

## 15. Impact on AI Drama System

- Reinforces canonical Story -> Episode -> Scene -> Shot.
- Supports preserving Shot as execution-level unit and Scene as narrative container.
